# Daily News — 25 October 2026

Hot finance and technology stories from Sunday, 25 October 2026. US stock markets were closed. **Bitcoin fell below $80,000 over the weekend, then recovered to about $80,800.** **Stock futures rose on Sunday evening** before a week with the Fed, the ECB, the Bank of Japan and Big Tech earnings. In tech, the week's security news was heavy: **two PHP bugs ("PHP2Shell")**, **a hacked certificate authority (eMudhra)** and **a ConnectWise breach**. A report said **Amazon has billions of dollars of Nvidia racks waiting for power**.

## Finance

### Crypto

- **Bitcoin fell to about $79,100–$79,150 on Saturday**, its lowest level in more than a month (Bitcoin.com, Investing.com).
  - It was back above $80,000 by Saturday afternoon (US time).
  - **Sunday: it recovered to about $80,800.** Later reports put it near $80,400.
- **Liquidations: more than $810 million in 24 hours** (Coinglass data). About $689 million were bullish bets.
  - Cryptotimes puts the total at $820 million.
- **Why it fell:**
  - Thin weekend trading.
  - Weak tech stocks. The Nasdaq fell 2.6% on Thursday, its worst day since 5 June.
  - High oil prices.
  - **US spot bitcoin ETFs lost about $773.5 million over two days** (Jinse Finance).
- **Support levels to watch:** about $78,400, then $76,000–$76,600.
- **Other crypto news** (Jinse Finance, via KuCoin):
  - **South Korea postponed its crypto tax.** It was due to start in January.
  - **Tether froze about 2.21 million USDT** in nine addresses linked to a CoinDCX security incident.
  - **The Keystone hardware wallet website was breached.** Customer names, addresses and order details leaked.

- **US stock futures rose on Sunday evening** (CNBC). S&P 500 futures were up about 0.4%.
  - On Friday, the Dow fell more than 650 points and the S&P 500 lost 0.5%.
  - The S&P 500 and Nasdaq had a second weekly loss in a row.
- **Oil eased a little early Monday (Asia time)** (CNBC):

  - WTI: −1.5% to about $101.83.

- **Also this week:** ECB and Bank of Japan decisions. Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon report earnings.
- **Goldman Sachs cut its 12-month KOSPI target by 25%** after the AI stock selloff (Jinse Finance, Investing.com).
- **Middle East markets fell** as the Yemen conflict grew. Egypt's main index dropped as much as 5% (Jinse Finance). Single source.
- **RBA Deputy Governor Hunter said Australia's rate hikes are "not yet over"** (Jinse Finance).

### AI returns

- **Apollo's chief economist Torsten Slok says AI has not paid off yet outside Big Tech** (note dated 24 October).
  - Revenue per worker and margins are rising for the "Magnificent 7".
  - They are flat for the other 493 S&P 500 companies.
- **Gartner estimate:** AI data center owners need about $8.5 trillion in revenue through 2030 for a 12% return. Its forecast is at most $3.6 trillion (via AI Brief).

## Technology

### Amazon's "dark GPUs"

- **Business Insider reports AWS expected about $5 billion of Nvidia GB300 racks** before sites were ready to run them (via AI Brief).
  - Some sites had space but not enough power. Finished racks waited in warehouses.
  - August emails projected about $3 billion more each month.
  - Amazon's capital spending is about $220 billion this year.
- **Amazon denies any problem.** It calls staging hardware near sites "best business practice".

&nbsp;

### Security

- **PHP2Shell: two PHP bugs allow remote code execution without login** (This Week in Security).
  - The newsletter says about 70% of internet-facing servers are affected.
  - Found by researchers at Calif. OpenAI and Wiz confirmed them.
  - The embargo lifted on 21 October. Finland and other governments took some websites offline as a precaution.
  - Amazon says it saw attacks a week before the public disclosure.
- **eMudhra, an Indian certificate authority, was hacked** and its private keys stolen.
  - Mozilla, Google and Apple now block its certificates.
  - One leaked key was simply "4", so it was easy to guess.
  - eMudhra is leaving the TLS certificate business.
- **ConnectWise's cloud backup app was breached.**
  - Attackers stole credentials for customers' Microsoft 365 backups.
  - Microsoft says at least 2,200 tenants were hit. It blames a China-aligned group.
- **Railway (cloud hosting) had customer secrets exposed.** It asks all users to rotate API keys and secrets.
- **Other breaches this week:** PingCap (TiDB) extortion claim, UpdraftCentral WordPress plugin backdoors, Staples gift card fraud (at least $5 million), Quest Diagnostics and State Bank of India.
- **DHS banned DeepSeek from federal civilian networks** (This Week in Security).

&nbsp;

### AI

- **OpenAI now trains its top models with no live internet access** (AI Daily Digest).
  - On 20 September, an internal research model reached the internet through a misconfigured DNS resolver.
  - OpenAI paused frontier training. It restarted on 1 October.
  - Training now uses an offline, cached copy of web content.
- **Anthropic gave its Agent Skills standard to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation** (AI Daily Digest).
  - More than 70 agent platforms support it, including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code.
  - The foundation also added OpenAI's Open Responses, Hugging Face's OpenEnv and Alibaba's OpenSandbox.
- **Anthropic says the junior share of its technical hires fell from 27% to 9%** since early 2025 (via AI Brief, The Guardian).
  - These are shares, not headcounts. Total technical hiring still grew.
- **Google announced Agent Compute Engine** on 22 October. Each agent runs in its own isolated microVM. Preview comes in the next few months.
- **AWS released "Infinity"**, an open-source (Apache 2.0) runtime for running many agents cheaply. An idle agent uses about 100 KB.
- **Nvidia expanded Cosmos** with new tools to build world models and synthetic training data.
- **Cursor agents can now control desktop apps**, such as the iOS Simulator, to test what they build.
