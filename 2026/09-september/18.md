# Daily News — 18 September 2026

Hot finance and technology stories from Friday, 18 September 2026. The week closed with two endings and one beginning: **Warren Buffett stepped down as Berkshire's chairman after six decades**, the **Bank of Japan raised its policy rate to 1.25%, a 31-year high** — and the yen fell anyway. In markets, **the 10-year Treasury yield pushed back to 5%** while **bitcoin broke above $80,000**. In tech, **Anthropic said Claude now leads 26% of the work that builds the next Claude**, and the three largest labs moved toward a **FINRA-style body to test models before release**.

- The real story was in rates and leadership, not in the index prints.

### Rates, currencies and commodities

- **10-year Treasury yield rose 6bp to 4.995%** — back at the 5% line it had just slipped under. The **2-year hit a new 52-week high at 4.741%**.
  - Traders are now pricing real odds of **another Fed hike in October**, two days after Wednesday's 25bp increase.
- **USD/JPY climbed to about 156.7**, and briefly above 157. A rate hike that weakens the currency is the week's clearest signal about how little the market believes in follow-through.
- **WTI crude fell 1.58% to $100.30/barrel**, extending the relief from the Saudi pipeline repair.
- **Gold rose 0.57% to about $4,425/oz.**
- **Diesel hit a record $6.29/gallon** — the energy shock is still working through the freight economy even as crude eases.

### Bank of Japan: the hike that didn't stick

- **The BoJ raised its policy rate 25bp to 1.25%**, the highest in **31 years** and its second increase this year.
- **The vote was 7–2** — and the dissent was read as dovish, not hawkish. Governor **Ueda gave mixed signals on the next move**, and markets took that as the end of urgency.
- Result: **the yen fell, 10-year JGB yields slipped, and the Nikkei 225 rose about 1.5%**, led by AI-linked shares. Exactly the opposite of a textbook tightening reaction.
- The takeaway: a split board at 1.25% puts **the whole Japanese tightening cycle in doubt**, which is why the currency moved the way it did.

### Buffett steps down

- **Warren Buffett, 96, stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway**, ending a run of roughly six decades.
- He becomes **chairman emeritus** and stays on the board. **His son Howard Buffett takes over as chairman.** **Greg Abel** continues as CEO, running a group with **more than $1 trillion in assets**.
- From his farewell letter on Abel: *"He has taken hold of the chief executive officer job in every respect."*
- Context that matters more than the ceremony: **Berkshire stock has lagged the S&P 500** through the transition year.

### Crypto

  - About **$183M of short positions** were squeezed out on the move.
- **Coinbase gained more than 10%**; crypto equities rallied broadly.
- Notable because it came **despite** the CLARITY Act stalling in the Senate earlier in the week and **despite** yields back at 5%. CoinShares still warned of a *"difficult setup"* into year-end.

## Technology

### Anthropic: Claude is building Claude

- Anthropic published an **R&D automation index**: as of August, **Claude leads 26% of the work that builds the next Claude**, up from **under 1% in February**. Over **90%** of that work is at "collaborator or better" level.
- Roughly **30,000 agents run at once** inside the company, with safety monitors **blocking about 1 action in 47,000**.
- Anthropic is also **installing embedded evaluators with employee-level access** to verify its safety practices and report incidents. **Sam Altman committed to placing third-party evaluators at OpenAI** as well.

### A self-regulator for frontier models

- **OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind** have spent weeks building a **FINRA-style standards body** to test powerful systems before release. OpenAI's policy chief confirmed it.
- Separately, **Trump weighed in against slowing AI development**, framing it as a race: whoever wins AI, wins.
- **Brookings and Fudan jointly proposed US–China "red lines"** — starting with explicit restrictions on autonomous nuclear weapons decisions — ahead of the Trump–Xi meeting.

### Models and benchmarks

- **OpenAI released Astra for Law**, a GPT-6 Astra configuration tuned for legal research and drafting. It is the company's **first vertical model edition**, and it shipped with a legal-research index spanning **230 million URLs**.
- **GPT-6 Astra scored 49.2% on Microsoft's ProgramDistill** benchmark for software engineering task reconstruction, against **Claude Opus 5 at 28.8%**.
- **Z.ai** said **GLM-5.3-Flash** now runs on **100,000+ Chinese accelerators**, with throughput **tripled in two weeks** and per-token costs comparable to Nvidia GPUs. The domestic-silicon gap is closing faster than the chip export rules assumed.
- **AutoArk's Edge0** ran a **35B-parameter model straight off SSD on a Mac Mini M4 Pro at 20.4 tokens/second** with minimal active memory — a real shift in what local inference costs.

### Power, chips and money

- **The House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417–3**, requiring large data centers to **cover the full cost of the grid upgrades they trigger**. A near-unanimous vote on AI infrastructure cost is a political signal worth noting.
- **Crusoe tripled its valuation to $30.9B.**
- **Jensen Huang said Nvidia expects to sell twice as many chips next year.**
- **India announced phase two of its semiconductor mission**: **$13.5B over 12 years**, including a **$5B Applied Materials commitment**.
- **Novo Nordisk** said it is using **Claude across drug discovery workflows**; **Emulate raised $700M at a $3.7B valuation**.

### Security and courts

- The **Strix agent found a three-year-old GitHub admin token** sitting in **Baseten's container registry**, with repository access. An AI agent finding a stale credential in a vendor image is the kind of supply-chain gap that is easy to miss and cheap to exploit.
- A **federal judge denied OpenAI access to confidential Apple settlement files** in the antitrust proceedings.

## What to watch

- **Does the Fed go again in October?** The 2-year at a 52-week high says the market is starting to believe it. That, not earnings, is the driver right now.
- **The yen.** A hike to a 31-year high that weakened the currency means the BoJ has a credibility problem, not a rates problem. Watch for intervention talk.
- **The 5% line on the 10-year.** It has been crossed in both directions twice this week. Equities are tracking it closely.
- **Berkshire without Buffett as chairman.** The stock already lags the index. Abel now owns both the operations and the narrative.
- **Who regulates the labs.** A FINRA-style body built by the three largest labs is self-regulation by the incumbents. The structure will matter more than the announcement.
- **Grid cost allocation.** A 417–3 House vote means the "who pays for AI's power" fight has broad agreement. Watch utilities and generator names.
