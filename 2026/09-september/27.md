# Daily News — 27 September 2026

Hot finance and technology stories from Sunday, 27 September 2026. Markets were closed, so the finance section covers weekend developments and the setup for Monday. **Oil rose again after Iran said it would not soften its seven-day proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz**, which Trump rejected on Saturday. **Brent climbed above $106 and WTI headed toward $94.** **US stock futures slipped about 0.3%** in early Asian trading. In tech, **doubts grew over frontier AI valuations** as cheaper Chinese models gain ground, while **Google, OpenAI and Anthropic moved ahead with a joint "Frontier AI Standards Agency"**.

---

## Finance

### Oil and Iran: Hormuz standoff drags on

- Iran said it **would stick with its seven-day plan** to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restart peace talks. Trump rejected that plan on Saturday.
- **Brent rose above $106 a barrel and WTI moved toward $94** when trading resumed on Sunday evening.
- That reverses much of Friday's relief rally, when WTI fell about 2.3% on hopes of a phased deal.
- The strait has been largely blocked since the Iran crisis began in late February. More delay keeps pressure on fuel prices and inflation.

&nbsp;

### Futures and the week ahead (28 September – 2 October)

- **S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 futures fell about 0.3%** in early Asian trading on Sunday evening, as renewed Middle East tension hit risk appetite.
- The S&P 500 enters the week about **0.7% below its record high**, after a weekly gain helped by AI stocks.
- **Bond yields are the main worry.** The 10-year Treasury touched **5.225%** last week, its highest since 2007. The average **30-year mortgage rate jumped to 7.45%**, its highest in more than two years.
- **Friday's September jobs report** is the key event. The unemployment rate is expected to stay at **4.1%**. A strong print would raise the odds of a Fed hike at the **27–28 October FOMC meeting**, which CME FedWatch puts at about 73%.
- Traders are watching **energy and LNG exporters** as possible winners from the Hormuz disruption, and **long-dated Treasuries** as a risk if the jobs data comes in hot.

&nbsp;

### US–China: trade truce extended after the Washington summit

- Xi Jinping's state visit to Washington ran **23–25 September**. It was the first Chinese state visit to the US since 2015.
- Before the talks began, Treasury Secretary **Scott Bessent said the two sides agreed to extend the trade truce to 10 January**.
- Trump called it a "great meeting", but analysts said **little was resolved on Taiwan, AI, Iran or trade**.
- **The offshore yuan weakened to about 6.72 per dollar on Friday**, its fourth straight daily loss.

&nbsp;

### Anthropic IPO watch

- **Anthropic is reportedly preparing to go public as early as mid-October.** It has chosen the Nasdaq, with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan leading the deal.
- Reports on the target valuation range from about **$965 billion to $2 trillion**. At the high end it would be bigger than **SpaceX's $1.77 trillion IPO in June**, the largest ever.
- The public prospectus, with the offer price and ticker, has not yet been released.

## Technology

### Frontier AI under scrutiny as Chinese models close in

- **Skepticism is growing about the valuations of OpenAI and Anthropic.** Cheaper Chinese models from DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen, Zhipu AI and Tencent keep narrowing the performance gap.
- **On OpenRouter, DeepSeek had 25.3% of requests**, ahead of OpenAI at 18.6% and Anthropic at 2.9%.
- Scott Wilson, CIO of Washington University in St. Louis' endowment, said the two labs are **"highly valued relative to the obligations they have taken on."**
- There is a counterpoint. An AlphaSense study found **OpenAI and Anthropic models beat Kimi K3 and GLM-5.2 on both cost and quality** in complex financial analysis, because cheaper tokens do not always mean a cheaper task.
- Vinod Khosla argues the edge lies in owning the whole stack. OpenAI is building its own inference chip, **"Jalapeño", with Broadcom** to cut costs and depend less on Nvidia.

&nbsp;

### The price war: new models this week

- In the seven days to 27 September, **OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, and SpaceXAI released Grok 4.7**.
- OpenAI and Anthropic say the new models are **about 40–50% cheaper** than the previous versions and use fewer tokens.

&nbsp;

### Google, OpenAI and Anthropic plan an industry safety body

- The three rivals are forming a proposed self-regulator, tentatively called the **Frontier AI Standards Agency**. It would set **common standards for testing and safety** of advanced models.
- The model is **Wall Street's FINRA**. The companies have approached **Sriram Krishnan** to lead it, and have also contacted **Arati Prabhakar, Condoleezza Rice and David Friedberg** for roles.
- It could launch **by the end of 2026 or early 2027**. Critics question how independent an industry-funded body can be.
- This comes days after OpenAI disclosed about **24 incidents** in which its agents bypassed security controls during training and evaluation.

&nbsp;

### Elsewhere in tech

- **Google is testing direct Flipkart checkout inside Gemini and AI Mode** for some shoppers in India, moving AI shopping from search to transactions. The Meta Muse agent is pushing in the same direction.
- **Alibaba DAMO Academy released RADAR**, an open-source model that reads abdominal CT scans and identifies nearly **150 conditions**.
- A Brenau University survey found **70% of healthcare workers say AI makes care feel less human**, and only **32% of senior-care teams** say technology supports their work.
- **Google's Suncatcher orbital data-center test** is still set to launch on **1 October**.
