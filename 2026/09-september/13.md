# Daily News — 13 September 2026

Hot finance and technology stories from Sunday, 13 September 2026. A weekend with no U.S. cash session, but two stories moved the tape anyway: **the CEOs of Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI all called for slowing frontier AI development**, and **Gulf–Iran talks on the Strait of Hormuz were postponed hours before they were due to start**. Equity futures opened the week lower on the AI warning; crude opened higher on Hormuz.

## Finance

### The day in one line

- Markets were closed. The news was not. A safety-first turn from the AI labs hit the single trade that has carried the year, and a postponed Hormuz summit put the risk premium back into crude.
- All of it lands two days before the **15–16 September FOMC**, where a 25bp hike is now close to fully priced.

### Weekend setup into Monday

- **U.S. equity-index futures fell** and Asian stocks retreated as trading opened, after the major AI labs called for a slowdown in the technology's development.
- The concern is narrow and specific: AI capex and AI-linked megacaps are the reason the S&P 500 is up double digits this year. A self-imposed brake on capability is a growth story with a new asterisk.
- No U.S. close to report — 13 September was a Sunday.

### Rates: the Fed meeting is effectively decided

- Friday's **August CPI** printed **+0.4% m/m** and **3.4% y/y**, both in line. **Core CPI came in at +0.3% m/m**, a tenth above consensus, with core at **2.4% y/y**.
- The core overshoot did the damage. **Odds of a 25bp hike jumped to roughly 87–90%** for the 15–16 September meeting, from the current **3.50–3.75%** target range.
- Context: the July FOMC held **9–3**, with three regional bank presidents dissenting for an immediate hike. Those three now look like the majority.

### Oil: Hormuz talks called off

- A **Gulf–Iran summit on Hormuz commercial transit, scheduled for Monday, was postponed late Sunday** without a new date.
- That follows weeks of escalation in the strait and reinserts a supply premium into crude just as the inflation data turns hawkish.
- Brent averaged **$91/bbl in August**, $7 above July, and spiked past **$108** in the second week of September on strikes against shipping.

### Commodities: resource nationalism widens

- **Multiple countries restricted exports of industrial tungsten over the weekend.**
- Tungsten is a small line item with a large blast radius — aerospace, semiconductor tooling, and heavy industry all depend on it and none can substitute quickly. Expect it to show up as a cost note, not a headline.

### Crypto

- **Bitcoin held around $77,200**, after touching **$79,890 on Saturday** and fading as hike odds firmed.
- The pattern is the familiar one: crypto is trading as a rates asset into the FOMC, not as a hedge.

### Index mechanics: SpaceX

- **SpaceX's weighting in the Nasdaq-100 is set to rise at the quarterly rebalance later this month**, forcing a fresh round of index buying.
- The driver is structural, not fundamental: a construction rule in the index combined with staggered lockup expiries has kept the tradable float unusually small since the July listing.
- SpaceX joined the Nasdaq-100 on **7 July**. **Russell 1000** entry is expected at the September or December reconstitution; **S&P 500** inclusion is blocked until at least mid-2027 on seasoning and GAAP-profitability rules.

## Technology

### The story: three AI CEOs ask for the brakes

- On **12 September**, Anthropic CEO **Dario Amodei** published an essay, *"We Must Pace the Frontier"*, arguing the industry must deliberately slow how fast it improves model capability.
- His framing: pacing is **not** a halt to training or technical progress. It is taking enough time to align and safeguard each model — and letting outside parties verify that the work was actually done.
- **Anthropic committed unilaterally to give third-party evaluators, including METR, permanent employee-level access** to its systems.
- **Sam Altman (OpenAI)** pledged to adopt the same "independent evaluators with employee-like access" standard. **Elon Musk (xAI)** wrote simply, "Dario is right."
- Why this is unusual: three direct competitors agreeing to a capability brake is a coordination move, and coordination among rivals on a lucrative product line is the kind of thing antitrust regulators normally ask about. The verification commitment — outsiders with insider access — is the part with real teeth.

### The pressure behind it

- **Jacob Coxon**, an AI researcher who resigned from Anthropic, told the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg on Saturday that people inside the labs are **"genuinely frightened"** about the pace of progress.
- His line from the interview: without a slowdown, he believes there is a strong chance of catastrophic outcomes in the near term.
- His viral resignation post preceded Amodei's essay by days. The sequence — insider resigns publicly, CEO publishes a pacing manifesto, rivals endorse it within 24 hours — reads less like coincidence than response.

### Agents keep shipping regardless

- **OpenAI's managed Agents API** is in public beta, exposing the Codex harness: session handling, orchestration, context compaction, crash recovery.
- **Salesforce launched seven named Agentforce agents** on 11 September — **Casey** (service), **Paige** (IT/HR), **Carter** (commerce), **Hunter**, **Marshall**, **Piper**, and **Fin** — six generally available, Hunter in pilot.
- **Hunter is the notable one.** It runs on a new **long-horizon runtime** that pursues a goal over weeks rather than a single chat session. That is a different product category from a chatbot, and a different risk profile.
- The tension is plain: the same week the labs asked for a slowdown on capability, the application layer shipped agents that run unattended for weeks.

### Privacy and security

- **LG televisions were reported to contain spyware**, the latest in a long line of smart-TV telemetry findings.
- Ongoing campaigns over the weekend: compromised sites serving malicious payloads, and continued phishing activity.
