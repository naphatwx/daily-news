# Daily News — 7 September 2026

Hot finance and technology stories from Monday, 7 September 2026. **U.S. cash markets were closed for Labor Day**, so the finance section covers the global tape that did trade — Asia, Europe, oil, gold and crypto — plus the rate-hike repricing carried over from Friday's hot payrolls print.

## Finance

### The day in one line

- No U.S. cash session. The action was in **oil**, in **rates expectations**, and in a mixed Asia/Europe tape.
- Two forces dominated: **Brent near $97** on the U.S.–Iran fight in the Strait of Hormuz, and **rising odds of a Fed hike on 15–16 September** after Friday's payrolls beat.

### Oil surges as the Hormuz fight escalates

- **Brent** traded around **$97 a barrel** on Monday — up roughly **9% over five days** and **19% over the month**, near a six-week high.
- **WTI** settled around **$92.6**, up about **1.3%** on the day, **+12.8%** over the month and **+48.8%** year over year.
- Trigger: the U.S. and Iran traded strikes over the previous 24 hours. Washington hit **three Iranian oil tankers**; Tehran struck tankers and fired a ballistic missile toward U.S. warships patrolling near the strait.
- Roughly **a fifth of global oil supply** moves through the Strait of Hormuz in normal times. Traffic has been disrupted for much of the past five months.
- This is now the main inflation risk in the September Fed debate — a supply shock arriving in the same week as U.S. CPI.

### Rate-hike bets keep building

- Friday's **August nonfarm payrolls: +162,000** against roughly **50–55,000** expected, unemployment steady at **4.1%**.
- Traders moved further toward a **Fed hike at the 15–16 September meeting**. Global bond yields climbed, and the dollar stayed firm.
- The Fed is in its **blackout period** — no official commentary until the decision.
- Notable cross-current: despite climbing yields, investors kept adding risk rather than cutting it.

### Gold gives ground

- **Gold** fell toward **$4,400/oz**, extending Friday's slide.
- Cause: hike expectations plus a firmer dollar. Higher real yields hurt a non-yielding asset, and that outweighed the safe-haven bid from the Middle East.

## Technology

### Startup funding: sovereign tech, hard infrastructure, vertical AI

Monday's funding tape tilted away from generic AI apps and toward physical infrastructure and regulated-industry AI.

- **Pixxel (India)** — **$100 million Series C**, the largest single round ever raised by an Indian space-tech startup. Hyperspectral earth-imaging satellites.
- **Jet Zero (Australia)** — **A$30 million** for a planned sustainable aviation fuel refinery, backed by major corporates.
- **Navana.ai (India)** — **₹40 crore** to scale voice AI for banks and other regulated enterprises.
- **Cato (Milan)** — **€6 million** to automate public-sector tender discovery and bidding.
- **Jaipur Robotics (Switzerland)** — **€4.3 million** for computer vision and automation in waste-to-energy, cement and biomass plants.

Read-through: capital is chasing **sovereign capability** (space, fuel, industrial automation) and **AI inside regulated workflows**, not another chatbot layer.

### Berlin ransomware leak lands

- The **Rhysida** group published almost **6 terabytes** — **1,439,893 files** — from Berlin's state administration on the dark web.
- Berlin's governing mayor **Kai Wegner** had refused the demand: **30 bitcoin (~€2 million)**. "The state of Berlin is being blackmailed."
- Data was stolen **7–12 August**; affected departments were not disconnected from the state network until **14 August** — a two-day theft window followed by a two-day gap before containment.
- Transport and environment departments were confirmed affected. The leak includes a folder labelled **"AG CBRN-Rahmenplanung"** — chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear threat planning.
- Rhysida, believed to operate out of Russia and Eastern Europe, has been active since May 2023 (British Library, Chilean Army, healthcare providers).
- The lesson is the timeline, not the ransom: detection and disconnection lagged the exfiltration by a week.

### The frontier-model week that just happened

September opened with the densest run of frontier releases this year, and the market was still digesting it on Monday.

- **Anthropic** — **Claude Fable 5.1** and trusted-access twin **Mythos 5.1** (1 September), plus a **75% cut to cache-read pricing**.
- **OpenAI** — **GPT-6 Astra** (3 September), the first model to trip its **critical-cyber** safeguard threshold. President **Greg Brockman** called it the start of the AGI era without formally claiming AGI.
- **Google DeepMind** — **Gemini 3.8 Flash** plus a defenders-only **Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber** with vulnerability detection and automated patching. Priced at **$0.75 per million input tokens / $3.75 per million output**.
- **Meta** — **Muse Spark 1.3**, blended price near **$0.10 per million tokens**.

Three patterns worth naming:

1. **Three of four launches ship a gated cyber tier.** Frontier capability and security gating are now shipped together.
2. **Pricing is a quarterly moving target** — promos, cancellations and scheduled doublings.
3. **Gains are coming from post-training environment scaling**, not new base architectures.

### Moonshot AI's listing sets a China AI benchmark

- Beijing-based **Moonshot AI** (Kimi models) filed **confidentially** for a **Hong Kong IPO** on 3 September, seeking **$3 billion** at a **~$50 billion** valuation from its ongoing round.
- Banks: **Goldman Sachs, CICC, Deutsche Bank**. Listing targeted as early as **Q1 2027**.
- Prior raise: over **$2 billion** from Meituan, China Mobile and CPE — total funding above **$5.5 billion**.
- This is the price marker for Chinese frontier AI in public markets.

### Chips and the AI build-out

- **Nvidia** and **Micron** kept catching institutional flows — semiconductor momentum refused to roll over even as industrial names stumbled.
- **Nvidia–Hugging Face** ($12.93B, its largest deal ever) is still moving through antitrust review — the first Nvidia AI deal that cannot be structured as a quasi-merger to avoid it.
- **Broadcom's** AI-chip revenue up **221%**, custom silicon gaining on merchant GPUs.
- Specialist silicon funding continues in the background: **SambaNova** closed **$1B** at an **$11B** post-money in July; **Groq** raised **$650M** in June.

### Apple: two days out

- **John Ternus's** first event as CEO — **"Surprise and shine"**, Wednesday **9 September**, 10:00 Pacific.
- Expected: **iPhone 18 Pro / 18 Pro Max**, Apple's **first foldable iPhone**, **Apple Watch Series 12**, **Watch Ultra 4**, possibly new AirPods.
- Apple was the worst large-cap performer in Friday's session (-2.55%) going into it.
